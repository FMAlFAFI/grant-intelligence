# experiment 3: cross-lingual similarity validity (arabic and english)

Generated by `python -m app.drift.cross_lingual` with the embedding model `Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B@97b0c614be4d` and the
translation model `Helsinki-NLP/opus-mt-ar-en@c5b2a50db78d`.

## what was tested

Two ways of comparing an Arabic text with an English one:

- **direct**: both texts are embedded as they are, with the multilingual embedding model.
- **translate-then-compare** (the baseline the brief asks for): the Arabic text is translated to English
  first, with a pinned local translation model, and the two English texts are embedded.

On two frozen sets:

- **Sentence retrieval.** 50 parallel Arabic and English sentences. They were written from a
  template of ten topics in five settings, so they are controlled test sentences, not proposals, and
  several sentences share a topic. Each sentence has to find its counterpart among the candidates.
- **Ranking on labelled sets.** The 19 labelled drift sets that pair an Arabic proposal abstract with an English
  publication title. The labels are analyst-written synthetic scenario labels
  (`docs/drift-calibration.md`). None of these sets is labelled divergent.

## sentence retrieval

| Method | Query to candidates | Sentences | Correct at rank 1 | Correct in the top 3 | Mean reciprocal rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| direct | Arabic to English | 50 | 49 of 50 (98%, 95% CI 90%-100%) | 50 of 50 (100%, 95% CI 93%-100%) | 0.990 |
| direct | English to Arabic | 50 | 50 of 50 (100%, 95% CI 93%-100%) | 50 of 50 (100%, 95% CI 93%-100%) | 1.000 |
| translate-then-compare | translated Arabic to English | 50 | 44 of 50 (88%, 95% CI 76%-94%) | 50 of 50 (100%, 95% CI 93%-100%) | 0.937 |
| translate-then-compare | English to translated Arabic | 50 | 48 of 50 (96%, 95% CI 87%-99%) | 50 of 50 (100%, 95% CI 93%-100%) | 0.977 |

| Method | Mean similarity, matching sentences | Mean similarity, other sentences |
|---|---|---|
| direct | 0.7389 | 0.5449 |
| translate-then-compare | 0.9164 | 0.7066 |

## ranking on labelled proposal and publication sets

9 aligned and 10 partially aligned sets. "Ranked correctly" is the share of
aligned-versus-partial comparisons in which the aligned set has the higher similarity.

| Method | Ranked correctly (95% CI) | Mean similarity, aligned | Mean similarity, partially aligned |
|---|---|---|---|
| direct | 100% (100% to 100%) | 0.64 | 0.46 |
| translate-then-compare | 89% (69% to 100%) | 0.80 | 0.62 |

For comparison, aligned sets in which proposal and publication are both in one language score 0.74 on average.

## what this means for stage 4

- **Order.** Direct comparison ranks 100% of comparisons correctly and translate-then-compare 89%.
- **Scale.** Aligned cross-lingual sets score 0.64 with direct comparison and 0.80 with translation first;
  same-language aligned sets score 0.74. Direct comparison is 0.10 below that scale and translation first is 0.06 above it. Neither method puts
  cross-lingual and same-language sets on one scale, so one pair of cut-offs cannot serve both.
- **Decision.** Stage 4 keeps direct comparison and reports every figure by language pairing. Translating
  first did not rank better here, and it adds a second model with its own errors (the translations are in
  `cache/translations/` and can be read). Both methods were measured on 19 labelled sets only.

## limitations

- Small sets: 50 template sentences and 19 labelled sets. Read the intervals.
- The ranking test has no divergent Arabic-proposal set, so it cannot show how either method treats one.
- The translation model was not evaluated on its own; a poor translation lowers the baseline, not the
  direct method.
